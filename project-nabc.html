NABC Mobile App

A wayfinding and contactless check-in app for the North American Bengali Conference, built to move thousands of attendees through entry and meals without the queues.

Product / UI-UX Design QR Check-in Wayfinding

Overview

NABC is an annual conference celebrating Bengali culture and connecting the diaspora community through cultural programs, food and shared events. At the scale of a multi-day conference, getting people through the door and fed without long queues is its own design problem — I led the UI/UX design of the companion app that attendees, volunteers and coordinators used to make that happen.

Objectives

Guide attendees

An easy, map-based way to find venues, stalls and checkpoints across the event space.

Streamline entry & food

QR codes for quick, contactless entry and food-packet collection instead of manual ticket checks.

Support families

Let one person manage check-in and food collection for a whole group — kids, parents, grandparents — from a single phone.

Equip volunteers

A dedicated scanning mode so volunteers could verify entries and meals quickly at peak times.

Key features

Interactive map & wayfinding — labelled zones and directions to entry points, food stalls and seating, so first-time attendees didn't need to ask around.

QR-based entry & food collection — every attendee got a unique code at ticket purchase, cutting wait times at both the door and the food line.

Master control for groups — a family holding multiple tickets could check in or collect food for any member from one device, without handing out separate phones.

Volunteer scanning mode — a minimal, fast interface built for verifying codes in real time under queue pressure, with little to no training required.

Challenges & how I solved them

Managing a group from one phone — the risk was letting one device control several entries without weakening security. A master-control dashboard let a user toggle between attendees' codes while keeping each check-in individually verified.

Keeping volunteers fast under pressure — the scanning flow was stripped down to the minimum taps needed, so a new volunteer could pick it up during the event itself.

Designing across very different comfort levels with tech — attendees ranged from tech-savvy young adults to elderly relatives, so the UI leaned on large, clearly labelled icons and step-by-step prompts rather than assuming prior app experience.

Results

The app noticeably cut entry and food-collection wait times through QR check-in, gave attendees a self-serve way to navigate the venue, let families manage multiple tickets without confusion, and made volunteer shifts easier to run at peak crowd times — the core measure of success for a conference this size.
