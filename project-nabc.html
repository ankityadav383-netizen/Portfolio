Project Overview

Celebrating culture, at scale

The event

NABC is an annual conference celebrating Bengali culture and connecting the diaspora community through cultural programs, food and shared events. At the scale of a multi-day conference, getting people through the door and fed without long queues is its own design problem.

My role

I took on the role of Product (UI/UX) Designer to build the companion app that attendees, volunteers and coordinators used to make that happen — tailored to a very wide range of ages and comfort levels with technology.

Problem Statement

The Core Challenges

  1. 01

    Queues at scale

    Getting thousands of attendees through the door and fed without long queues is its own design problem.

  2. 02

    Every age, every comfort level

    The audience spans tech-savvy young adults to elderly guests with limited tech experience.

  3. 03

    Families on one phone

    Groups with children or elderly attendees need to manage several entries and meals without handing out separate phones.

  4. 04

    Volunteers under pressure

    Volunteers need a straightforward, quick-to-navigate interface, particularly during peak times.

Objectives

What the App Had to Do

  1. 01

    Guide Attendees

    An easy, map-based way to find venues, stalls and checkpoints across the event space.

  2. 02

    Streamline Entry & Food Distribution

    QR codes for quick, contactless entry and food-packet collection instead of manual ticket checks.

  3. 03

    Enhance Usability for Families

    Let one person manage check-in and food collection for a whole group — kids, parents, grandparents — from a single phone.

  4. 04

    Enable Volunteer Assistance

    A dedicated scanning mode so volunteers could verify entries and meals quickly at peak times.

Users

Two apps, one event

App 01

Attendee

Everyone who comes to the event, from tech-savvy young adults to grandparents.

  • Find sessions, stalls and checkpoints
  • Get in with a QR pass
  • Collect meals, one QR per meal
  • Manage a whole family from one phone
App 02

Volunteer

The people at entry points, the food counter and the registration kiosk, often at peak times.

  • Scan a QR or type a registration number
  • See who the pass belongs to at a glance
  • Mark food packets in one slide
  • Keep a log of every check

Scan, verify, record

Key Feature

User Navigation with Maps

Find it without asking

An interactive map let users navigate the event venue with ease. Labelled zones and directions helped attendees locate entry points, food stalls, seating areas and other amenities — even on their first visit.

Filters and live status

Filter sessions by day, type and location; every card shows Live, Delayed or Expired.

Map and schedule, one tap away

A floating button opens the venue map or the day’s schedule from anywhere in Events.

Key Feature

QR Code-Based Access

One code, two jobs

Every attendee received a unique QR code at ticket purchase. It doubled as an entry pass for swift, contactless access and as a food voucher, cutting wait times on both lines.

A pass for every meal

Breakfast, lunch and dinner each get their own themed QR, with passes left and used on the card.

Key Feature

Master Control for Multiple Tickets

Manage a whole group

This addressed the needs of families and groups, particularly those with children or elderly attendees. A single device could manage multiple entries and food collections without handing out separate phones.

Swipe between people

The pass header reads “Ankit Yadav + 1” with the number of passes; dots let you move between each attendee’s QR.

Key Feature

Volunteer Module

Built for the queue

Volunteers got a dedicated interface for scanning QR codes at entry points and food stalls, with real-time verification that a code was unique and valid for its assigned entry or meal slot.

Learn it in minutes

Two big tiles, one scan area and a slider: designed to be picked up during the event itself, with minimal training.

Design Approach

Designing for Every Attendee

  1. 01

    User-centric design

    Given a diverse user base spanning every age and tech comfort level, the design emphasized simplicity, readability and accessibility above all.

  2. 02

    Seamless user flow

    From onboarding to QR presentation, every interaction needed minimal taps to reach maps, codes, or the master-control dashboard.

  3. 03

    Volunteer interface

    Built for clarity and speed, so a new volunteer could get scanning without extensive training.

Problem Solving

Managing Multiple Tickets on One Device

Challenge

Ensuring users could efficiently control multiple entries and food collections from one device without compromising security.

Solution

A master-control dashboard where users could toggle between different attendees’ QR codes, reducing complexity while maintaining security checks per person.

Problem Solving

Smooth Volunteer Operations & Diverse Users

Challenge · Volunteer speed

Volunteers needed a straightforward, quick-to-navigate interface, particularly during peak times.

Solution

A minimalistic UI enabling fast access to scanning and real-time validation, cutting attendee wait times.

Challenge · Diverse demographics

The app had to work for tech-savvy young adults and elderly attendees with limited tech experience alike.

Solution

A simplified UI with large, clearly labelled icons and step-by-step prompts made the app accessible to everyone.

Results & Impact

What Changed

  1. 01

    Faster entry & food collection

    QR-based check-in noticeably cut wait times at both the entrance and the food line.

  2. 02

    Self-serve navigation

    The interactive map gave attendees an easy way to find their way without asking staff.

  3. 03

    Simplified group management

    Families and groups could manage multiple attendees with ease via the master-control feature.

  4. 04

    Smoother volunteer operations

    Volunteers could assist attendees more effectively even during peak crowd times.
